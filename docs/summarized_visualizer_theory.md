# Visualizer Theory — Summary Note
 
Condensed overview of *The Visualizer as a Formal System* (full doc: "ethereal jungle visualizer theory"), a companion to *Ethereal Jungle as a Formal System*. Every section §-references the music doc; the recurring move is "nothing new is needed" — the same theory, one sense organ over.
 
**The shared-bus thesis** comes first and everything hangs off it: the visualizer shouldn't listen to the audio (FFT-reactive visuals are lossy inference of things the composition already knows), it should read the same control signals the music's generators read — `T(t)`, drift, wildness, mode. That makes it clairvoyant (it can foreshadow the drop, which a reactive visualizer literally cannot do) and makes it a limb of the same will, per the music doc's §4 architecture.
 
From there: a **mapping dictionary** grounded in cross-modal correspondence research, a visual stream vector with the same bimodal-clustering rule as the audio mix, and a synch-point economy (Chion's synchresis, priced by Barlow indispensability — including the claim that a visual downbeat is a metric anchor, so visuals can license higher wildness in the drums). Then a **taxonomy of six families** (growth, fields, reaction-diffusion, self-similar, corpus/video-chop, artifact operators) with an affordance table showing why no single family suffices — rhythm affordance and ground affordance are nearly disjoint, so a complete scene needs a figure generator plus a ground generator, which is the two-stream theory again. **Transition theory** mirrors the music doc's §6: a distance metric, a ranked continuity layer (camera motion signature wins), a visual seam operator, and the same crossfade-produces-four-streams failure.
 
**Open proposal to stress-test before building:** the one-world solution (§4.4) — rather than switching visualizers, one continuous jungle where each family is a biome and transitions are camera travel, with altitude bound to mode brightness (phrygian roots, lydian sky). It is the visual twin of the music doc's "one tonal center per set" degenerate solution, and the thesis argues the two should be adopted together. This is the biggest commitment the future concrete document must accept or reject — it constrains everything downstream.
